# Wietse's Content Buckets — Full Detail

These are the 8 defined content lanes for Wietse's LinkedIn presence. Each bucket has sub-topics and angles. When selecting topics, use this as a menu — not a checklist. The goal is variety across buckets over a 4-week window.

## 1. Responsible AI in government (no hype, high trust)

Wietse's positioning: where AI genuinely helps in civic engagement, stated honestly and without hype.

Sub-topics:
- **Adoption**: where to start (policy, process, people)
- **Use cases that actually save time**: analysis, reporting, triage, routing
- **"Checkable AI"**: auditability, human-in-the-loop, transparency
- **The hard conversation**: bias, privacy, legitimacy, failure modes

Angle guidance: Wietse is credible here because Go Vocal actually ships AI features. The posts should show what responsible AI looks like in practice — concrete, specific, unsentimental. Avoid the two common traps: AI hype ("AI will transform democracy!") and AI doomerism ("AI will destroy democracy!"). The truth is more interesting and more useful.

## 2. Participation as decision infrastructure

Wietse's core intellectual territory — treating participation as something governments build into their operating system, not bolt on as a PR exercise.

Sub-topics:
- **What governments are *really* optimising for**: legitimacy, speed, fairness, trust
- **Designing for representativeness**: hybrid by default, beyond the usual suspects
- **Closing the loop**: decisions people accept (even when they disagree)

Angle guidance: This is where Wietse's systems-level thinking shines. Trace the cascade — show second and third-order consequences. Connect to real examples from Go Vocal's 600+ government deployments. The insight here is that participation is infrastructure, not a nice-to-have.

## 3. Polarisation + deliberation (beyond surveys)

The "what's next" bucket — where participation methodology is evolving.

Sub-topics:
- **Citizens' assemblies and when they work** (and when they don't)
- **Digital deliberation**: what's promising vs what's performative
- **Mini-publics vs mass participation** ("mini + maxi public") and how to combine them

Angle guidance: Wietse has strong, informed views here. The field has a lot of romanticised thinking about deliberation — Wietse's value-add is being practical about what works in context, at scale, with real governments that have real constraints.

## 4. Methods playbook (doing participation right)

Practical, actionable content for participation practitioners. This bucket has strong engagement potential because the audience can immediately use what they read.

Sub-topics:
- **Practical tips & patterns**: what works when teams have 2 FTE
- **Choosing formats**: surveys vs proposals vs PB vs assemblies
- **Field-tested templates/resources**: repurpose from Go Vocal's content bank

Angle guidance: Keep it concrete and humble. Frame as "here's what we've seen work" not "here's the right way." The audience for this content is practitioners who are overworked and under-resourced — respect their constraints.

## 5. Impact & experimentation in the field

Show, don't tell. Real outcomes from real places.

Sub-topics:
- **"What we tried / what we learned" loops**: honest about failures too
- **Hidden stories**: small town wins — participation isn't just for big cities
- **Inspiration + benchmarks**: Bloomberg-style city innovation examples

Angle guidance: This is where customer stories live, but framed through the lens of learning rather than promotion. Open with the outcome, give enough context to feel real, quantify where it adds clarity, and extract the transferable principle.

## 6. Founder integrity + long-term entrepreneurship

Personal but not private. Wietse's reflections on building a mission-driven company.

Sub-topics:
- **Building with constraints**: focus, tradeoffs, saying no
- **Mission + governance**: B Corp lens, incentives, values under pressure
- **Co-founder / partner dynamics**: what makes it work
- **Triplets parenthood / boundaries**: keep it "personable, not personal"

Angle guidance: These posts work because they're rare (1 per month max) and because they're tied to principles, not emotions. The audience doesn't want a founder diary — they want wisdom from someone who's built something meaningful over a decade. Always land the lesson.

## 7. Culture hooks that teach (pop culture analogies)

Short, memorable reframes tied to a serious takeaway.

Sub-topics:
- Use a movie, show, book, or cultural moment as a lens for a governance/participation insight
- Always land the lesson back in government reality

Angle guidance: Use sparingly — max 1 per month. These are pattern-interrupt posts that earn new followers. The analogy should be genuinely illuminating, not forced. If you have to explain why the analogy works, it's the wrong analogy.

## 8. "Screenshots from the field" (show, don't tell)

Raw, real moments from participation practice that reveal patterns.

Sub-topics:
- Anonymised town hall moments / prompts / questions that reveal a pattern
- "This is what people actually ask" → what it means for engagement design

Angle guidance: These are high-engagement because they feel authentic and invite practitioners to share their own experiences. Keep commentary minimal — let the "screenshot" do the talking, then draw out the principle. Works well as short posts (under 150 words).
