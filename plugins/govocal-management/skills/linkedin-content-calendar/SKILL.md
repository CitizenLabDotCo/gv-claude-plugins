## Drafting posts

When drafting a LinkedIn post — whether triggered by the calendar, a direct request, or raw notes from Wietse — follow this process.

### 1. Load the thought leadership voice

Before writing, read the `wietse-thought-leadership` skill at the path where it's installed and follow its voice, tone, and structural guidance. Every post must sound authentically like Wietse.

### 2. Follow the mandatory post flow

Every post must follow this structure (from the Founders' Socials Hub strategy):

1. **Hook** — 1–2 lines that earn "see more" through insight, not hype
2. **Stakes** — what breaks, costs time, or erodes trust if this isn't addressed
3. **Shift** — a reframing, trade-off, or calm contrarian insight
4. **Proof** — a concrete example, observation, or learning (focus on what was learned, not what was sold; quantify only if it adds clarity)
5. **Takeaway** — 1–3 working principles, trade-offs, or hypotheses being tested in practice (not a polished playbook)
6. **Invite** — a peer-level question that invites nuance, disagreement, or alternative approaches

This flow is non-negotiable. It's what makes Wietse's posts feel like peer conversations rather than broadcasts.

### 3. Format for mobile-first LinkedIn

- **First line is everything.** LinkedIn truncates after ~140 characters. The opening must earn the "see more" click. Never start with "I'm excited to share..." or "Last week I had the pleasure of..."
- **1–3 sentences per paragraph, generous whitespace.** Dense blocks get skipped on mobile.
- **No hashtags unless Wietse explicitly requests them.** The hub strategy says no hashtags by default.
- **No emojis** — or only minimal, necessary ones.
- **No buzzword soup.** Write as if speaking with peers and partners, not to an audience.
- **Tag people and organisations** when referencing them — boosts reach and signals engagement.
- **If Go Vocal is mentioned**, frame it as part of a broader ecosystem — alongside customers, partners, and peers. Go Vocal should never be the hero of the post.

### 4. Deliver the full package

For each draft, provide:
- **The publish-ready post**
- **2 alternative hooks** (clearly different angles)
- **1 contrarian angle** (1–2 lines that could stand alone as a post or comment)
- **1 "comment to test" question** (sharper and more polarising than the main invite, but still respectful)

If Wietse provided a draft rather than raw notes: preserve his meaning and strong lines, don't rewrite unless it improves clarity or authority, and edit ruthlessly for focus and tone. Add a short "What I changed and why" section (3–6 bullets).

### 5. Save the draft to Notion

Create (or update) the entry in the Founders Social Calendar:
- Set **Status** to `Draft to Review`
- Set the **Post Date**, **Founder** (`Wietse`), **Post Type**, and **Key Message**
- Write the full draft (post + alternative hooks + contrarian angle + comment-to-test) into the **page body**

## Performance feedback loop

After a post has been live for 48+ hours, if Wietse asks to review performance or if this is part of a scheduled check:

1. Ask Wietse how the post performed (or check if he's shared metrics in Slack/Notion)
2. Note: engagement quality > vanity metrics. Track comments and DMs over likes and impressions.
3. Flag patterns: which buckets generate the most conversation? Which hooks drive the most "see more" clicks? Which post types get shared?
4. Feed learnings back into the next Friday's topic selection — double down on what's working, experiment with what's not.

Over time, build a mental model of what resonates with Wietse's specific audience (public sector practitioners, partners, KOLs) and use it to sharpen topic selection and hook writing.

## Guardrails

- **No partisan politics.** Democracy and governance discussions are core to Wietse's content, but never take sides on specific parties, elections, or political figures. The content is about systems and processes, not political actors.
- **No product-first posts.** Unless it's a big release, Go Vocal should never be the subject of a post. If it appears, it's as proof in a broader lesson.
- **Reader-first. Clarity > cleverness.** No gimmicks, no engagement bait, no trend-jacking.
- **Be personable, not personal.** Avoid forced vulnerability. The founder integrity bucket is about lessons and principles, not diary entries.
- **Strong opinions, stated calmly.** Wietse's contrarian takes land because they're measured, not because they're loud.
