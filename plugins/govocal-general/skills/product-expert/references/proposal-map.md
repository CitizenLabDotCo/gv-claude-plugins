## The narrative arc (sequence answers/proposals this way)

1. **Personal letter first** — exec summary signed by the CEO: admiration for the
   prospect's commitment → their objectives mapped to our solutions → social proof (600+
   governments; Copenhagen/Vienna/Seattle; matched references) → a named expert who
   "personally oversees" implementation.
2. **Prove you listened** — requirements table + live demo environment *before* product prose.
3. **Who we are = mission + values-as-credentials** — democracy mission, B Corp, 50/50
   gender balance, awards, then hard numbers.
4. **"Your needs: what we understood" → "Our solution: why Go Vocal"** — restate the RFP,
   answer with exactly **four numbered differentiators**: ① Complete participation toolbox
   ② Powerful workflows and CMS ③ Rich, high-quality insights ④ Expert guidance, global
   network — cross-linked to requirement numbers.
5. **Partnership before product** — the four success pillars (leadership vision, internal
   structure, skills & resources, digital infrastructure).
6. **Product story is a workflow, not a feature list** — "1. Configure › 2. Engage ›
   3. Manage › 4. Decide". Front simple for engagement, back office powerful for efficiency.
7. **Engage is the IAP2 ladder** — Inform → Consult → Involve → Collaborate → Empower,
   each umbrella with an italic one-line tagline; the **inclusive engagement suite (360
   Input / FormSync / ECHO) sits before the ladder** with the thesis "traditional
   platforms force a false choice: lose offline voices or transcribe them by hand."
8. **Decide closes the loop**; ethics box pre-empts AI objections.
9. Services (named people) → optional roadmap → references → price → onboarding →
   technical annexes last. Commercial decision-makers first, technical evaluators last.

## Writer workflow (the SOP + guidelines, condensed)

1. Mirror the prospect's language from the tender publication (set the variables; then
   sweep for missed instances).
2. Copy RFP requirements into the requirements table; link each to "Our solution: why Go
   Vocal" by number; close the loop back with page numbers + heading hyperlinks.
3. Adapt Our solution to the requirements; bold key phrases; delete the marked
   "DELETE AFTER – INSPIRATION" text.
4. Choose references matching the client profile; weave "similar organizations used
   feature X" through Our solution.
5. Pricing: pick custom-offer or standard-plan table; break down extras that could be
   dropped; end the exec summary with why-us + succinct pricing intro.
6. Remove roadmap mentions if unused (cmd+F); delete the guidelines page and changelog.

## Writing rules (from the SOP's product-section guidelines)

- **Lead with problems, not features.** Weak: "The Representativeness Dashboard allows you
  to compare demographics." Strong: "Understanding whether you're hearing from your whole
  community or just the usual voices is critical for legitimacy. The Representativeness
  Dashboard compares…"
- **Calibrate depth to tier**: critical differentiators (AI, deliberative tools) 3–5
  paragraphs w/ examples + strategic framing; workflow features 2–3; standard features 1;
  table stakes (SSO, backups) 1–2 factual sentences.
- **Second person, benefits over mechanics**; concrete scenarios over abstractions
  ("When you tell council 'residents under 35 prioritize transit significantly more than
  older cohorts,' you're backed by statistical evidence, not anecdote.").
- Professional-but-conversational tone, peer-to-peer with a city administrator.
- Structure: H1 major sections, H2 subsections (page-break before long H2s), H3 detail;
  no blank space around titles ≤H3.
- NorthAm tone rule: "community members" and "organization", not "residents" and "city".
