# Wietse's CEO-to-Mayor first-touch voice

These Gmail drafts are the reason this skill exists. The 10 Notion dossiers are for the team; the 10 drafts in Wietse's inbox are what actually puts Go Vocal in front of the buyer.

## The voice in one paragraph

Wietse writes like a founder who's been in this space for a decade and has met a lot of mayors. Not salesy. Direct, warm, specific. He names the signal concretely (tender number, budget line, quote from the press release) because vagueness reads as a mass-mail. He offers a 20-minute conversation, not a demo. He assumes the reader is busy and smart, so he gets to the point in paragraph two. The signoff is plain and personal — his first name and a single-line title.

If an email has a buzzword, it's not Wietse's. If it uses the word "synergies" or "best-in-class" or "digital transformation journey", it's a bot.

## Structural pattern (always 4 short paragraphs, ~120 words)

1. **Open with the specific signal.** Quote or cite it. Make it unmistakable that this email was written for this city, not a template.
2. **One sentence on why Go Vocal is paying attention.** This is where our relevance lives — and where a less sharp email would brag. Wietse names one concrete fact: a comparable city, a feature that maps directly to what they published, a number.
3. **The ask.** 20-minute conversation. Not a demo, not a meeting, not a discovery call. A conversation. Two or three specific questions he'd want to ask them, framed as his curiosity, not our qualifying.
4. **Signoff.** "Best, Wietse" + "Co-founder & CEO, Go Vocal" on one line. No P.S. No "looking forward."

## What to include

- Name the mayor or procurement officer by first name if known — otherwise "Dear [Mayor/Madam Mayor]" is fine.
- Cite the source: "I read in [BOAMP/La Gazette/your strategic plan published on Tuesday]..."
- Name one comparable Go Vocal city of similar size and context. *Only* name a real reference — the sales team can verify. If you're unsure, say "cities at your scale" and don't name specifics.
- One concrete example of what we helped that comparable city do. Not a feature list — an outcome.

## What to avoid

- Product feature lists. Wietse never sells features in a first-touch.
- "I came across your recent..." — too passive. Say what he actually read.
- "I wanted to reach out to..." / "Hope you're well" — dead words.
- Promises of ROI or % improvements.
- "circle back," "touch base," "synergies," "digital transformation journey," "engagement best practices"
- Any P.S. — Wietse's first-touches don't have them.
- More than 130 words. If you're at 140, cut something.

## Three sample drafts

### Sample 1 — Tender signal

> Subject: Lyon's participation platform tender — quick note from Brussels
>
> Dear Mayor Doucet,
>
> I saw Lyon's BOAMP publication last Friday for the citizen participation and participatory budgeting platform. The scope — ideation, deliberation, participatory budget for €180k over two years — maps almost exactly to what Rennes launched with us two years ago.
>
> A few reasons I'm paying attention: our work in Rennes started with participatory budgeting and grew into the city's primary consultation layer. Similar scale to Lyon, similar political appetite. The lessons from that rollout would be directly useful as you evaluate vendors.
>
> Could I ask for twenty minutes? Three things I'd want to understand: your participation team's current tooling, how you want to handle multilingualism at neighbourhood level, and what "good" looks like at the end of year one.
>
> Best,
> Wietse
> Co-founder & CEO, Go Vocal

## When there's no verified contact

If you can't find a direct email for the mayor or the procurement officer, do not leave `to:` empty without explanation. Instead, in the first line of the body, write:

> *[Suggested recipients: Mayor [name] (primary), [name] (Head of Procurement, backup). No verified email — AE to confirm via LinkedIn / municipal site before sending.]*

Then continue with the standard 4-paragraph body. Wietse edits the `to:` before sending.

## Reference links to embed in the body

When relevant, embed ONE link only — usually to a concrete Go Vocal case study in the comparable city referenced. Never to the Go Vocal homepage. Never to a generic product page. The link must earn its place.

## Final check before saving the draft

Re-read the draft with these questions:

1. If I removed the city name, would the email still work for any city? If yes, it's too generic — rewrite paragraph one.
2. Did I name a reference city? Is it real? Is it a comparable size?
3. Am I over 130 words? Cut.
4. Is there a buzzword? Cut.
5. Is the ask a "conversation" (correct) or a "meeting/demo/call" (wrong)?

If any answer is "no good," rewrite before saving the draft.
